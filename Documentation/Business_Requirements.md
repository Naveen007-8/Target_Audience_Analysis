# Target Audience Analysis
## Data-Driven Credit Card Customer Acquisition Strategy

**Project Domain:** Banking and Financial Services (BFSI)  
**Project Type:** End-to-End Data Analytics  
**Tools:** Python, Pandas, NumPy, SQL, Power BI, DAX, Excel, GitHub

---

## 1. Business Background

The banking industry operates in a competitive environment where multiple banks offer credit cards with different benefits, reward programmes, fees and credit limits.

A bank wants to improve its credit card customer acquisition strategy by understanding customer demographics, income profiles, occupations, credit characteristics and spending behaviour.

The bank needs data-driven insights to identify promising customer segments and develop more focused marketing strategies.

## 2. Business Problem

The bank does not have a sufficiently clear analytical understanding of which customer profiles demonstrate promising spending behaviour and may be suitable for targeted credit card marketing.

Marketing activities that are not aligned with customer needs may result in inefficient campaign spending and missed acquisition opportunities.

The bank therefore needs to analyse its available customer and transaction data to identify potential target audiences.

## 3. Project Objective

Analyse customer demographics, annual income, occupational profiles, credit characteristics and transaction behaviour to identify promising customer segments for credit card marketing.

Develop data-driven recommendations that help the marketing team prioritise customer segments, understand spending preferences and consider suitable customer acquisition approaches.

## 4. Business Questions

### Customer Demographics
1. Which age groups contain the largest number of customers?
2. Which age categories demonstrate higher transaction activity?
3. How do customer profiles differ between City and Rural locations?

### Income and Occupation
4. Which occupations have higher average annual income?
5. Which income bands represent the largest customer segments?
6. Which occupation and income combinations demonstrate promising spending behaviour?

### Spending Behaviour
7. Which customer segments contribute the highest transaction value?
8. Which product categories are popular among different age groups?
9. Which customers have higher transaction frequency and average transaction value?
10. How does customer spending change over time?

### Credit Profile
11. How do credit scores and credit limits vary across customer profiles?
12. What relationships exist between annual income, outstanding debt and credit utilisation?

### Customer Acquisition Strategy
13. Which customer segments should the marketing team investigate first?
14. What product benefits may be relevant to different customer segments?
15. Which marketing channels should be considered for further testing?
16. What additional competitor and campaign data is needed to evaluate acquisition effectiveness?

- Actual campaign conversion predictions without response labels
- Actual marketing ROI calculations without campaign cost and outcome data

## 10. Assumptions and Limitations

The available datasets may represent customer and transaction activity but do not automatically establish whether a customer has a credit card from another bank or is willing to accept a new offer.

The project will distinguish between observed transaction behaviour and inferred marketing opportunities.

Age and income analysis will be descriptive and used to understand aggregate customer segments, not to make individual credit eligibility decisions.

Competitor positioning, campaign performance and customer conversion will require additional validated data.

## 11. Expected Business Outcome

The final analysis should help the bank understand:

- Which customer segments demonstrate promising transaction behaviour.
- Which age, occupation and income groups merit further marketing investigation.
- Which product categories are relevant to different customer segments.
- How marketing propositions and channels could be tested.
- What additional information is required to measure actual acquisition success.

The final recommendations will be supported by reproducible analysis and clearly documented evidence.
